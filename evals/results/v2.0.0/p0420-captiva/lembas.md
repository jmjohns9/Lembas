**Problem.** P0420 means "Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold, Bank 1." The engine computer compares the oxygen sensors before and after the catalytic converter. When the rear sensor's signal swings almost as much as the front one, the converter is no longer storing and using oxygen, so it is treated as worn out. The 2.4L Ecotec in the 2014 Captiva is known for burning oil, and that oil can coat the catalyst and wear it out early.

**Fix.** Rule out the cheaper causes before you replace the converter, which costs roughly $600 to $1,500 or more:
1. Look for misfire codes (P0300 to P0304), which point to coils or plugs. Check the oil level and how fast it drops.
2. Check for exhaust leaks ahead of the rear O2 sensor. A ticking noise or soot near the manifold or flex joint is a sign.
3. With a live-data scanner, watch the rear O2 sensor at warm idle. It should stay fairly steady around 0.6 to 0.7 V. If it switches quickly like the front sensor, the cat is bad. If it sits flat or stuck, suspect the sensor itself (about $50 to $100).

To confirm the repair, clear the code and drive about 100 miles of mixed driving, then check that the code stays off.

**Alternatives.** Replace only the rear O2 sensor if the live data shows it lazy or stuck. Fix the oil burning first if consumption is above about 1 quart per 1,000 miles, because otherwise a new cat will fail again.

**Why this fix.** Checking first can save you buying a converter you don't need. It is safe to keep driving for now if the check-engine light is steady, the engine runs smoothly, and you smell no sulfur or hear no rattle. Stop driving if the light flashes, which means active misfires that can overheat the cat and become a fire risk. The car will also fail an emissions inspection until this is fixed.
