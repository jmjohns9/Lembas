P0420 means the engine computer thinks the catalytic converter on bank 1 is no longer cleaning exhaust well enough. It sees this when the downstream oxygen sensor's signal starts to look like the upstream sensor's.

It's usually safe to drive for a while if the check engine light is steady and the car runs normally. Stop driving soon if the light flashes, the engine misfires or loses power, or you smell sulfur or burning. A misfire sends unburned fuel into the converter, which can overheat it and, in bad cases, start a fire. You'll also fail an emissions inspection while the code is set.

The most likely causes, in order:
1. A worn catalytic converter, which is common at this age and mileage.
2. A faulty downstream oxygen sensor, which costs much less to replace.
3. An exhaust leak ahead of that downstream sensor.
4. Oil burning. The 2.4L Ecotec engine in many Captivas is known for oil consumption from stuck piston rings, and the burned oil poisons the converter.

Check your oil consumption before buying a new converter. If the engine is burning oil, a new converter will fail again.
