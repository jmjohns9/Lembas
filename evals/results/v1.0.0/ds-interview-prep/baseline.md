Congrats on landing the interview. A week is enough time to prepare well if you focus on what data science interviews actually test. Your BI background is a real advantage here, so a big part of the job is framing what you already do in data science terms.

**1. Find out the format first**

Ask the recruiter what the loop looks like if you haven't already. Most DS interviews mix some of these: SQL, Python coding, statistics and probability, machine learning concepts, a case study or business problem, and a behavioral round. Knowing the mix tells you where to spend your week.

**2. SQL (likely your strongest area, so keep it light)**

Do a handful of timed problems to stay sharp on window functions, CTEs, self joins, date handling, and aggregation with conditional logic. Practice talking through your approach out loud, since that's what they grade.

**3. Python and pandas**

Be comfortable cleaning and reshaping data in pandas: groupby, merge, pivot, handling nulls, and basic feature engineering. If the role is coding heavy, do a few easy to medium LeetCode style problems with lists, dicts, and strings.

**4. Statistics and probability**

This is where BI candidates usually get probed hardest. Review:
* Hypothesis testing, p values, confidence intervals, Type I and II errors
* A/B test design: sample size, power, what can go wrong (peeking, novelty effects, multiple comparisons)
* Common distributions and when they apply
* Bayes' theorem with a quick worked example

Your math background helps, so focus on explaining these plainly rather than relearning them.

**5. Machine learning fundamentals**

Be ready to explain, in simple language:
* Linear vs. logistic regression and how to interpret coefficients
* Tree models, random forests, gradient boosting
* Bias and variance, overfitting, regularization, cross validation
* Metrics: precision, recall, F1, ROC AUC, and when you'd pick one over another
* Handling class imbalance and data leakage

Interviewers care more about judgment ("why this model, how would you know it's working") than formulas.

**6. Case and product questions**

Practice a structure: clarify the goal, define the metric, identify the data you'd need, propose an approach, and say how you'd validate and communicate it. This is where your experience shines. You've spent years turning messy business questions into dashboards people actually use at scale. Say that.

**7. Prepare your stories**

Pick 3 or 4 projects and write out the problem, what you did, the technical choices, and the measurable result. Good candidates from your work: anything with Python automation, statistical analysis behind a dashboard, or platform decisions affecting thousands of users. Have a crisp answer ready for "why move from BI to data science?" Also prep a couple of STAR stories on conflict, ambiguity, and influencing stakeholders.

**8. Research the company and role**

Read the job description closely and map each requirement to something you've done. Look at the company's products and think about what data problems they likely have. Prepare 3 or 4 thoughtful questions for them.

**Suggested week**

* Day 1: Format, job description mapping, write out your project stories
* Day 2: Stats and A/B testing
* Day 3: ML concepts and metrics
* Day 4: Python and pandas practice, a few SQL problems
* Day 5: Two or three case questions out loud, timed
* Day 6: Mock interview (a friend, a recorded run, or I can play interviewer)
* Day 7: Light review and rest
